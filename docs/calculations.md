# Calculation reference

This describes the current deterministic engine, not a forecast or a complete PayPal ledger. The seller's source data and optional inputs determine what can be calculated. Amounts are rounded to two decimal places for output; intermediate calculations generally use unrounded values.

## Coverage and period

The engine selects the currency with the largest total gross from eligible sales. Other currencies are listed with their eligible gross sales and excluded; no currency conversion is performed. It requires at least one eligible sale. An eligible sale is a positive sale, invoice payment, or subscription payment without a pending, failed, denied, refused, declined, cancelled, or unpaid status. The same exclusion applies to refund, reversal, and dispute rows. A sale marked refunded can still be counted as gross sales; its separate eligible refund is subtracted.

Rows are deduplicated by transaction ID, kind, calendar date, currency, and gross amount. For otherwise matching events, a settled record is preferred to an unsettled one. This preserves a sale and refund that share an ID. The source must still contain enough detail to distinguish genuinely different events with matching fields.

The reported period runs from the first through the last month containing an eligible sale, refund, reversal, dispute, or settled separate fee row in the selected currency. It includes intervening calendar months even when they have no activity. Invoice records do not extend this period. The last three months and the prior three months are consecutive slices of that period, including the end month even if it is still in progress. The engine does not infer missing months beyond the supplied records. `period_summaries` contains whole-period totals and buyer measures; last-three-month and prior-three-month summaries appear only with at least three and six observed calendar months respectively. `trend_delta` is last three minus prior three for each money-map amount, without extrapolation.

CSV parsing drops withdrawals, payouts, transfers, currency conversions and holds. Rows with unusable dates or currencies are skipped. Slash-formatted dates are read as month/day/year unless the first component is greater than 12, in which case it is read as day/month/year. Quantity must be a positive whole number and defaults to one when absent. A row is one order even when it contains more than one unit; mixed-item cart allocation is not established by this representation.

## Observed order categories and item groups

`business_shape` counts eligible order rows and their gross separately for the normalized `sale`, `invoice_payment`, and `subscription_payment` types. These are observed transaction categories, not a guess about what the seller makes or their intent. A source that does not label invoice or subscription payments cannot establish their true share.

`item_groups_by_period` reports gross sales, order counts, and refund amounts by named group (or `Unlabelled items`) for each month and, where available, the last three and prior three months. Refunds are assigned to their event month; the monthly objects are separate from the flat money-map rows.

## Money map and buyers

For each month, and then summed over the period:

- **Came in** = gross eligible sales.
- **PayPal fees** = absolute fee amounts on eligible sales. Separate fee rows are excluded when their relationship to sales fees is unknown, to avoid counting the same fee twice. A coverage gap is shown; the total cannot be assumed to include every fee.
- **Refunds and reversals** = absolute gross of eligible refund, reversal and dispute rows, using absolute net when gross is zero. They are recorded in the event month, including months after the last sale.
- **Item costs** = supplied per-item cost × quantity for each eligible sale. A named item-specific cost takes priority over the supplied average. Missing costs contribute zero to the arithmetic and are identified as missing, not established as zero.
- **Ad spend** = supplied monthly amount for each month in the period. If not supplied, it is excluded and a gap is noted. The interface normally starts it at zero.
- **Kept** = came in − sale fees − refunds and reversals − included item costs − included ad spend. It is not a complete profit figure when any cost or fee is missing.

An identified buyer is one with a buyer key. Overall returning buyers are identified buyers with more than one sale in the supplied data; the overall returning share is their count divided by identified buyers. Orders per buyer is identified sales divided by identified buyers. Average order is all eligible sales gross divided by eligible sale rows. Top-item share is the largest item group's gross divided by gross sales; top-buyer share is the largest identified buyer's gross divided by gross sales. Missing buyer identifiers make these buyer figures incomplete.

Each monthly row also has order and identified-buyer counts, returning and new buyer counts, returning share, average order, and orders per identified buyer, using the same window definition below. For a three-month window, the engine counts distinct identified buyers who bought in the window. A buyer is returning if they bought earlier in the data or bought more than once within that window. The window share is returning buyers divided by identified buyers in that window. Best share is the maximum over consecutive three-month windows in the observed period. `month_buyers` is distinct identified buyers in the final month; `past_buyers` is distinct identified buyers before that month. A first-seen buyer is new only relative to the supplied data, not necessarily new to the business.

## Findings

Monthly findings are ordered by their estimated monthly amount. A finding below 2% of average monthly gross sales is labelled “small, leave it for now.” These are what-if calculations, not proof of the cause or of recoverable revenue.

- **Returning buyers (L1):** With at least three months and identified buyers, use the best observed three-month returning share when it exceeds the latest three-month share. Otherwise use a hypothetical increase of five percentage points, capped at 99%. Hold the latest window's new-buyer count fixed. Target returning buyers = new buyers × target share ÷ (1 − target share); extra orders = max(0, target returning buyers − current returning buyers). Monthly estimate = extra orders × whole-period average order × whole-period kept share ÷ 3, assuming one average order per extra returning buyer. It is omitted if the kept share is not positive.
- **Unpaid invoices (L2):** A separate quick win, not a monthly estimate. In supplied transaction records, the engine uses explicit invoice IDs and recognized invoice statuses, retaining the latest observed state per invoice. It sums the absolute gross, or absolute net if gross is zero, of observed open invoices whose recorded date is at least 14 days before the analysis time. A later observed paid state prevents an earlier open state from being counted. The `sent` count covers distinct invoice IDs with any explicitly observed sent status, even if a later record says paid or unpaid; `current_sent` counts only latest observed sent states. `observed_issued` counts distinct IDs explicitly observed as sent, unpaid, paid or completed, without claiming the date or method of issue. Counts and age describe supplied observations, not necessarily all invoices; the latest record's date may not be the original issue date. Average observed days to payment is supplied only for invoice IDs with an explicit observed sent/unpaid date and a later or equal paid date; it averages the elapsed days across those matched invoices and reports their count. It is an observed interval, not necessarily time since invoice issuance, and excludes invoices without both records. The sandbox invoice-list route can supply its own separately sourced quick win using sent invoices and their outstanding balances.
- **Refunds on one item (L3):** For named item groups, total eligible refunds in the last three months; choose the largest group and divide its total by 3 and by 2. This models a 50% reduction. A refund lacking an item name can inherit it only from a unique original sale identified by its reference transaction ID. An observed item order refund rate is included only if every refund in the window links uniquely to a named sale in that same window: distinct referenced refunded orders for the chosen group divided by eligible sale rows for that group. Multiple refunds for one order count once. It is conditional on the supplied records being complete; otherwise no rate is given.
- **Small-order fees (L4):** For a confirmed US account in USD, inspect sales in the trailing 12 months below $26.67. Include only fees within $0.02 of 3.49% + $0.49, adding 1.5% for known international orders. Sum `0.40 − 0.015 × gross` and divide by 12. Show at $5 per month or more. Orders already paying the lower micropayment rate are excluded. Currency or buyer location alone does not establish the seller's account country.
- **Sets:** Require explicit cart details for every observed sale in the 90-day period; item titles alone do not establish cart contents. At least 60% of those carts must contain one piece. Derive up to three pairs from the same cart or an identified buyer's orders within 30 days. Estimate single-piece orders per month × 10% × 90% of the observed second-piece price, less supplied item cost when available. Show only at $20 per month or more.
- **Held money:** Include only payments dated within 30 days with an explicit confirmed current hold and explicit absence of tracking. A pending transaction is not sufficient. PayPal documents T2107 as a payment-hold event and T2108 as a release; an event alone does not establish current unreleased status or that tracking is absent. Transaction Search and CSV adapters currently do not establish all these facts, so this detection is restricted to sources that do.
- **Cross-border fees (L5):** No estimate is produced: the normalized data does not establish the extra cross-border portion of fees. Country alone does not establish it.
- **One-item dependence (L6):** A named top group is a risk if its whole-period gross exceeds 50% of gross sales. It has no monthly money figure.
- **Below-cost items (L7):** For each named group with supplied item cost, add `max(0, item cost × units + sale fee − sale gross)` for each sale, then divide by the number of months in the observed period. This is the observed shortfall, not a price recommendation. A break-even price at a new price is not calculated without a fee schedule that supports it.
- **Fewer first-seen buyers (L8):** With at least six months, compare counts of distinct first-seen buyers in the last three months and the three before. If the latter is larger, estimate `(previous count − recent count) × whole-period average order × max(0, kept share) / 3`. This is relative to the available history and assumes one average order per buyer.
- **Failed subscription payments (L9):** Only explicitly marked failed subscription-payment rows in the last three months are included. Their absolute gross amounts are summed and divided by three. This is observed failed payment value, not verified permanent loss or recovery. Payment rows alone do not establish the number of active, cancelled, or suspended subscriptions; the engine reports that gap.

A finding requiring unavailable data or a playbook target is not made up. In particular, unlinked item refund rates, a price-dependent break-even price, complete invoice history, cross-border fee differences, and subscription status totals require additional evidence.

## Synthetic sample and checks

The downloadable `public/sample-paypal-activity.csv` is fictional. Regenerate it from the deterministic demo with `node --import tsx scripts/generate-sample-csv.ts`. The CSV format does not carry the demo's structured carts or verified hold and tracking flags. The demo uses a fixed analysis time of 31 October 2026. Tests include hand-calculated money-map, returning-buyer, fee, cart, hold, refund-period, duplicate-event, and invoice-state examples.

## Editable what-if assumptions

`returningBuyerTargetPercent` optionally replaces the returning-buyer share target (0–99 inclusive); lower targets produce zero extra estimated orders. `refundReductionPercent` optionally replaces the 50% refund reduction assumption (0–100 inclusive). Both reject non-finite and out-of-range values. Empty values retain the original defaults. The browser and sandbox input route use the same bounds. These assumptions change estimated opportunities, not observed sales or money kept.

Equal-money monthly findings use a fixed ease approximation because the playbook has no effort scores: failed subscription payments, item refunds, small-order fees, one-time buyers, below-cost items, fewer new buyers. This order is an implementation judgment, not measured seller effort. Unknown finding IDs sort after these, alphabetically.
