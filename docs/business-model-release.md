## Upload interruption and deletion

New upload stages use random per-upload keys kept only in the encrypted journal. A seller can recover a complete interrupted stage without supplying the original file; an absent stage is abandoned and its attempt ID is retired. Workspace or source deletion can preempt an in-flight stage, invalidate its journal reference and key, and prevent a late writer from publishing it. A late request can still leave encrypted orphan ciphertext in Blob after the initial cleanup; without its erased random key it is inaccessible to the application. The app attempts cleanup when a stale writer observes a conflict, and an owner can run bounded cleanup of retired upload IDs while signed in. Local tests cover a simulated late artifact and removal; they do not establish provider backup erasure, cleanup after account access has ended, or the absence of a still-running writer. Cleanup readback is a point-in-time result. Legacy uploads use the original index and encryption and are deleted through the retained compatibility path.

## Interpretation and coverage

Business and product assignment is explicit. Matching titles, account identity and judge access do not verify a merchant. Old ambiguous uploads stay unassigned until the seller confirms their business and source. Existing historical summaries are not proof of settlement when their status is absent. Per-order fee summaries use matched recorded fees only for complete single-item cart evidence or an explicit dated seller confirmation scoped to the selected uploaded files. Other eligible orders use the disclosed rate derived from known same-currency shop fees, with recorded, estimated and unattributed coverage shown. For mixed or incomplete carts with a matching line value, the estimate applies that rate to the selected line; the unallocated shared charges and other item costs remain a gap. The card and explicit explanation use the same selected-item cohort and calculation. Source-file deletion invalidates its scoped confirmation. Monthly partial views count supported same-currency events, identify missing statuses, fees and quantities, and do not allocate mixed-cart charges without evidence. They are not a complete business profit statement. Confirmed monthly help is deducted once only when the seller confirms full-month coverage; otherwise it remains outside the amount. Owner time with zero cash pay is not valued as an economic cost. Approximate answers remain labelled as estimates. Explicitly confirmed ranges retain both endpoints, scope and effective dates; bounded calculations use those endpoints without midpointing and round interval endpoints outward; exact points retain the existing rounding convention. A range that may include 100% waste leaves materials unresolved. Monthly help ranges are included once only with full-month coverage and applicable dates.

Corrections retain prior facts and use their stated effective dates. Saved monthly versions preserve a calculation and input digest while its sources remain. Source deletion removes dependent views and import assignments; independently confirmed seller facts remain. The journal and export are bounded; the app refuses a write at the limit rather than silently evicting history.

The monthly view uses an explicitly confirmed trading start, evaluated on the seller-local date, to distinguish a pre-trading month from missing records; observed earlier activity is surfaced as a conflict. This does not assert that every recommendation uses trading start. The local synthetic fixture-copy workflow covers the named maker, reseller and short-history files, interruption recovery and repeated assignment without converting ambiguous legacy zero values. It is a test workflow, not an automated migration of customer data.

PayPal event codes T1108 and T1109 are recognized as separate fee-adjustment rows, not merchandise refunds. Their reported signed amounts remain outside the partial calculation because their balance effect has not been established; the interface labels them as unreconciled. See PayPal’s [transaction event-code reference](https://developer.paypal.com/reports/reference/t-codes/) and [transaction detail definition](https://developer.paypal.com/api/transaction-search/v1/definitions/transaction_detail/).

The broader semantic conversation/model evaluation is Phase B and has not been performed. The explicit cost explanation is not evidence that arbitrary paraphrases are understood. No new model call or PayPal financial write is required for this Phase A path.
